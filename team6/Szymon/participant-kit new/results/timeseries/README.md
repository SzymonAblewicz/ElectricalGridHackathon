# results / timeseries

**Three files, same shape: 980 branches × 168 hourly snapshots.**

| file | unit | size |
|---|---|---|
| `flows.csv` | MW, signed | 3.22 MB |
| `loading.csv` | fraction of `s_nom` (1.0 = at rating) | 3.34 MB |
| `signs.csv` | +1 or −1 | 0.43 MB |

Rows are branch names, columns are timestamps — `2030-01-14 06:00:00` through
`2030-01-21 05:00:00`, one week, hourly.

**This is the only part of the results that is snapshot-dependent.** The
matrices in [`../ptdf/`](../ptdf/) are not.

---

## flows.csv

Stage 4 output:

```
p_eff = p + K B φ
f     = PTDF · p_eff − B φ
```

Positive means power flows `bus0 → bus1`, on the arbitrary orientation from
`../network/branches.csv`.

**Verified against PyPSA's independent `n.lpf()` DC solver across all 168
snapshots: max difference 3.9 × 10⁻⁹ MW.** That is the strongest check in this
work — two separate implementations agreeing to numerical precision.

**The `− B φ` term matters.** The network has 2 phase-shifting transformers, one
at 17°. Drop the term and the flow on the most heavily loaded circuit in the
network, `3581-89516-1`, comes out at 44 MW instead of 123 MW against a 123 MVA
rating — 36% loaded instead of 100%. It stops looking congested at all. 272 of
980 branches are wrong by more than 1 MW, worst case 78.8 MW.

---

## loading.csv

`|flow| / s_nom`. A value of 1.0 means at the rating.

Ten branches reach 1.0 at some point in the week; the table is in
`../network/README.md`. 20 branches exceed 0.80.

Remember `s_nom` is TYTFS `RATE1`, a **planning** number. "100% loaded" means
100% of a planning rating, not of an operational limit.

---

## signs.csv

`sign(flow)`. Integer +1 or −1 — no zeros: a flow of exactly zero is mapped to
+1 so it cannot silently blank a whole sensitivity row (`np.sign(0)` returns 0,
which would multiply the row away).

**This is the file that makes any hour reconstructible:**

```python
sens_at_hour = ptdf_wind[branch] * signs.loc[branch, "2030-01-16 16:00:00"]
```

The full 980 × 157 × 168 signed product would be 25.8 million values. It is
never built, because `ptdf_wind.csv` (3.35 MB) and `signs.csv` (0.43 MB)
reconstruct any slice of it exactly.

### How stable is the sign

Measured over hours where a branch is above 50% loaded — idle branches flip on
numerical noise and would poison the statistic:

- **0.986** mean agreement with each branch's own peak-hour sign
- **0.874** worst case, `T54640-54630-1`
- **1.000** for eight of the ten congested branches
- 410 of 980 branches never flip at all

So a constraint group built at one representative hour is defensible. That was
an assumption earlier in this work; this file is where it was checked. Drawn in
`../../figures/new figures/3_congestion.png`.

## Where the underlying hours come from

**The 168 hourly profiles are synthetic**, generated by the kit authors — a
spatially correlated Gaussian wind field, a panel model for solar, and a winter
weekday demand shape scaled per bus by that bus's TYTFS load. They are anchored
so the scenario hour reproduces the TYTFS state.

**No individual hour in them ever happened.** They are not a historical year and
not a forecast.

The dispatch behind the injections came from PyPSA/HiGHS optimising this network
under the kit's placeholder marginal costs (gas 90, hydro 1, imports 150, wind
and solar −1 EUR/MWh).

**Consequence:** these three files, and every loading and sign statistic derived
from them, are the part of the results that changes when real EirGrid generation
data is substituted. `../ptdf/` does not change at all.

---

## Caveats

- **Dispatch-down here is not curtailment** in the EirGrid sense. No SNSP limit,
  no inertia constraint, no unit commitment. Do not compare against a published
  curtailment figure.
- **Intact base case only** — no contingencies. Real limits usually bind
  post-fault, so the real congested set is larger than 10.
- Renewables bid −1 EUR/MWh in the kit's cost set, which is deliberate but means
  only *differences* between two objective values carry meaning.
