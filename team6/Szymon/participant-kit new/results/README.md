# results

Ten CSVs in four folders (~45 MB), plus two interactive viewers and nine plots
in `sensitivity/`. Each folder has its own README.

**Start with
[`sensitivity/viewers/ptdf_viewer_withOverlaps.html`](sensitivity/viewers/ptdf_viewer_withOverlaps.html)**
— open it in a browser. The whole 980 × 157 matrix, sortable and filterable,
plus live overlap scoring with an adjustable threshold. No server, no internet.

Regenerate all of it with:

```
python ptdf_all.py --full     # the CSVs                    (~90 s)
python explore_ptdf.py        # viewers + figures 01-03
python overlap.py             # figures 04-09 + node_scores.csv
```

All three live in `team6/Szymon/`. The first step is the slow one — a 754×754
pseudoinverse, then a 168-hour dispatch optimisation with HiGHS.

## The network these describe

`WP2033_all-island` — winter peak, 2033 scenario. **The only one of the kit's
eight networks with a wind fleet**: 425 wind farms at 157 buses, 12,701 MW. The
other seven carry one token wind generator or none, so they cannot answer a
wind-curtailment question at all.

|---|---|

## The pipeline that produced these

Stages 1–4 of `team6/Szymon/shift-factor-ptdf-report.md`:

```
1.  K = incidence,  B = diag(1/x),  φ = phase shifts,  L = K B Kᵀ
2.  L⁺ = pinv(L)
3.  PTDF = B Kᵀ L⁺                                    → ptdf/
4.  p_eff = p + K B φ
    f     = PTDF p_eff − B φ                          → timeseries/
    sensitivity = PTDF[e,:] · sign(f[e])              → sensitivity/
```

`sensitivity` is what you want for "does curtailing here help"; `ptdf` is what
you want as a stable network property. They differ by one ± per branch.

**Stages 1–3 are pure network** — topology and reactance only. They are the same
for every hour of the year, and they do not change if you swap in different
generation data.

**Stage 4 is the only snapshot-dependent step**, because `sign(f[e])` depends on
what is generating and consuming that hour.

## Verification

Every one of these numbers was checked before being written:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| flows vs PyPSA's independent `n.lpf()`, all 168 snapshots | **3.9 × 10⁻⁹ MW** |
| `1ᵀp` (injections must balance) | 6.4 × 10⁻¹² |
| `1ᵀp_eff` (balance survives the phase-shift term) | 6.2 × 10⁻¹² |
| PTDF row sums (must be zero — injecting 1 MW everywhere moves nothing) | 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ |

The first is the one that matters: it compares our analytic route against a
completely separate DC solver. It proves the **arithmetic**, not the model.

## Provenance — where every number came from

**Nothing here was invented, estimated, or filled in.** Three tiers:

**1. From the kit, ultimately from EirGrid's TYTFS 2024 study files**
Bus list, voltages, line and transformer reactances, ratings (`RATE1`), phase
shifter angles, DC link parameters, installed generator capacities, load levels,
substation names and coordinates.

**2. Generated by the kit authors, not measured**
The 168 hourly wind and solar availability profiles and the demand shape. These
are synthetic — a spatially-correlated Gaussian wind field anchored so the
scenario hour reproduces the TYTFS state. **No hour in them ever happened.**
Carrier labels (`wind`, `solar`, …) were also inferred by the kit authors from
bus-name prefixes; about a fifth of capacity is labelled `unknown`.

**3. Computed here by `ptdf_all.py`**
`K`, `B`, `φ`, `L`, `L⁺`, `PTDF`, `p`, `p_eff`, flows, loading, signs, and every
summary column. The dispatch behind `p` comes from PyPSA/HiGHS optimising the
kit's network under the kit's costs.

**Which files are affected by tier 2:** everything downstream of the dispatch —
`timeseries/`, the `sensitivity` column, and every loading statistic in
`network/branches.csv`. **`ptdf/` is not.** Swap in real EirGrid generation and
the PTDF matrices are byte-for-byte identical; only the signs and the congested
set move.

## Limits — read before quoting a number

- **DC approximation.** No reactive power, no losses, no voltage. It cannot
  represent voltage-stability constraints at all, and several real EirGrid
  constraint groups exist for exactly that reason.
- **Intact base case only.** No contingencies. Real limits usually bind post-fault
  (N−1), so the true congested set is larger than the 10 branches found here.
- **Ratings are planning numbers.** `s_nom` is TYTFS `RATE1`, not an operational
  limit. A real control room works to seasonal and dynamic limits.
- **Dispatch-down here is not curtailment** in the EirGrid sense. This model has
  no SNSP limit, no inertia constraint and no unit commitment. Do not compare a
  number from here against a published curtailment figure.

The kit's own `README.md` LIMITATIONS section is the authority on all of these.
