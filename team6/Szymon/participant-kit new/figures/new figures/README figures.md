# README — figures

Three figures, generated by `figures.py` (in `team6/Szymon/`) from the CSVs in
`participant-kit new/results/`. Nothing in `figures.py` recomputes anything —
every number drawn here comes out of a results file.

Regenerate with:

```
python ptdf_all.py --full     # recompute results/
python figures.py             # redraw these three PNGs
```

The kit's own six figures live next door in `figures/participant kit figures/`.
Those are unmodified kit output; these three are ours.

---

## Conventions shared by all three

**Palette** is the kit's own `plotstyle.py`, so these sit beside the kit's
figures as one set rather than clashing with them.

**Blue is wind. Orange is other generation. Red is a problem** — an overload,
a sign flip, or a monitored circuit. Red is never used for an ordinary series.

**Signed quantities use a diverging ramp with a pale middle.** The ramp is
*reversed* from the kit's default: positive sensitivity means curtailing there
**relieves** the circuit, and relief should not be the alarm colour. So blue
relieves, red worsens.

**Three buses are never drawn.** The far terminals of EWIC and Greenlink and
the Scotland end of Moyle carry coordinates `(0, 0)`, which is in the Gulf of
Guinea. Left in, they stretch the map until Ireland is a dot. They are the same
three AC-isolated buses that make the Laplacian's nullspace four-dimensional
instead of one. No wind sits on them.

## 1_network.png — what is in the grid

Answers "what am I actually working with", before any physics.

### The map

**Nodes are coloured by class, not by voltage.** Every bus falls in exactly one
of four:

| class | count | what it is | why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **wind node** | 157 | one or more wind farms | what you curtail — the `n` in `PTDF[e,n]` |
| **other generation** | 108 | thermal, solar, hydro, interconnector | sets the base flow, and picks up the slack when wind is curtailed |
| **load only** | 102 | demand, no generation | the sinks that decide which way power flows, and so the sign of every sensitivity |
| **junction** | 387 | neither generation nor load | see below |

Classes are exclusive and assigned in that priority order: a bus with both wind
and a gas turbine is drawn as a wind node.

**Junctions are not one thing.** Of the 387:

- **210** are 3-winding transformer star points — the fictitious middle node of
  a real three-winding transformer. Not a place on the ground.
- **131** have degree ≥ 3 — genuine branching hubs where power splits between
  routes. Delete one and flows redistribute wrongly.
- **88** are dead-end stubs of degree 1. Verified: their maximum flow across all
  168 hours is `8.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ MW`, against 1,039 MW on the rest of the network.
  They carry nothing, ever. They are kept because removing them saves nothing
  measurable and risks introducing a bug.

**All dots are the same size.** Generation nodes are drawn slightly larger than
load-only nodes and junctions, but within a class every node is identical. The
map therefore reads **position and class only** — nothing about a dot encodes
capacity.

This is deliberate. Installed capacity spans **1,648 : 1** (0.5 MW to 824 MW),
and any sizing that fits that range on a map is either invisible at the bottom
or overwhelming at the top. Capacity is shown on the bar chart to the right
instead, where it gets a real axis with real numbers.

**Only transmission lines are drawn as edges. Transformers are not.**

This is deliberate and it is the correct choice. A transmission line is a route
between two places and has a length on the ground. A **transformer sits inside a
single substation** — it steps 220 kV down to 110 kV on one site. It has a
location but no route, so there is nothing to draw a line along.

Earlier versions of this figure did draw them, and the result was a handful of
dashed lines wandering across open country that meant nothing at all. Every one
was an artifact: a transformer's two busbars had each been given a *different*
inferred position, so a device inside one substation appeared to span 43 km of
the midlands. That is now gone.

**Transformers are still shown, where they belong.** Of the 10 congested
branches, 4 are transformers — including the second-worst constraint in the
network — so they are marked as a **red ring on the substation they sit in**.
Two windings of the same three-winding transformer collapse to one ring, so 4
congested transformers appear as 3 sites.

**Red lines** are the 6 congested transmission lines.

Two properties of the transformers that look wrong and are not:

- 114 of the 225 connect two buses at the **same** voltage. Those are busbar
  couplers, not step-downs.
- 20 have **negative** reactance. All are legs of a 3-winding star equivalent,
  where a negative leg is standard and correct.

**Node positions are snapped per substation.** Buses sharing a station name are
drawn at one point, and each 3-winding star point is placed on the terminals it
connects (`snap_stations` in `figures.py`). Only 5 of 616 station groups were
spread by more than 1 km, so this changes almost nothing else. It touches
drawing only — `results/network/buses.csv` keeps the original coordinates.

**What is still approximate:** 248 of 751 node positions were never geocoded and
sit at the mean of their neighbours. The lines between them are therefore drawn
in roughly the right place, not exactly. Longest drawn line is 104 km, median
6 km, which is plausible for Ireland — but never read a distance off this map.

### Right-hand panels

**Nodes by class and voltage** shows the split that the map's colour cannot:
wind is almost entirely 110 kV, while junctions skew to 220 kV and above,
because that is where the big three-winding transformers and the branching hubs
are.

**Where the wind sits** ranks the top 15 wind nodes by installed MW, with the
number of separate farms at each in brackets. The largest — Dublin Array,
W_Arklow_Off, the Codlings — are single offshore farms. Onshore nodes aggregate:
Boggeragh has 7 farms, Bootliagh 11, and one node carries 17. That aggregation
is why a "wind node" is the right unit here: every farm behind a bus shares the
bus's shift factor exactly.

## 2_sensitivity.png — one monitored branch

The same map geography, asking a completely different question.

**Figure 1 colours nodes by *class* — a category. Figure 2 colours them by
*value* — one column of physics.** Figure 2 also draws **only the 157 wind
nodes**; the other 594 are irrelevant to a curtailment decision and would only
add clutter.

### Left: the map

Colour is `sensitivity` for the single monitored branch, which is
`PTDF[e, n] × sign(f[e])` — the Stage 4 output. **Positive means curtailing at
that node takes MW off this circuit.** All 157 dots are the same size, so colour
is the only variable — the eye is not asked to trade off two encodings at once.

The monitored branch is drawn in red and labelled.

The picture is a real check on the maths: for `3581-89516-1`, the
Northern Ireland tie, everything dark blue is in Donegal and Tyrone, right
behind the circuit. The maths has no coordinates in it — the geographic
coherence is emergent, not built in.

### Right: the ranked curve

Every wind node sorted by `|sensitivity|`, highest first. **This curve is the
constraint-group decision.** Two reference thresholds are drawn, and the node
count each would admit is annotated.

The knee is the point. Sensitivity falls off a cliff after roughly 15 nodes,
which is why a threshold works at all — there is a genuine gap between "this
circuit sees you" and "it doesn't". Where exactly to cut is a judgement, and
EirGrid's own thresholds are not published, so the 5% and 10% lines here are
illustrative reference points, **not a standard and not a recommendation**.

The monitored branch is picked automatically as the one with the most hours at
its rating. Change it by editing the `monitored` line in `figures.py`'s `main()`.

## 3_congestion.png — the week

Two stacked panels, same x-axis: 168 hourly snapshots, ticked every 24 hours.

### Top: loading hour by hour

Every branch that reaches its rating at least once, ordered by how many hours it
spends there. Colour is loading as a percentage of rating; **red squares mark
the hours actually at the rating**, so an overload is never carried by colour
alone.

Row labels read `branch · L or T · hours at rating`.

**4 of the 10 are transformers.** `T3662-36671-1` binds for 75 hours — more than
every line except one. A model that carried only "transmission lines" would miss
40% of the constraints in this network, which is the practical argument for
carrying transformers as edges.

### Bottom: flow direction stability

The honest check on Stage 4. `sign(f[e])` is snapshot-dependent, and the
sensitivity sign inherits from it — so if the sign wandered, a constraint group
built at one hour would be wrong at another.

Each dot is one hour. **Blue** = same sign as that branch's own peak-loading
hour. **Red** = flipped, *and* the branch was above 50% loaded at the time.
Hours below 50% are excluded from the red test deliberately: a branch carrying
0.2 MW flips sign on numerical noise, and counting those would make a stable
network look unstable.

Result: agreement is 1.000 for eight of the ten and 0.874 at worst
(`T54640-54630-1`), 0.986 on average. **The sign is stable, so a constraint
group built from one representative hour is defensible.** That was assumed
earlier in this work; this panel is where it got checked.

### If you want capacity-scaled dots instead

Two places in `figures.py`. In `figure_network`, replace `size = 22 if ... else 9`
with:

```python
if name == "wind node":
    size = 14 + 78 * np.sqrt(sel["wind_MW"] / placed["wind_MW"].max())
elif name == "other generation":
    size = 14 + 52 * np.sqrt(sel["other_gen_MW"] / placed["other_gen_MW"].max())
else:
    size = 7
```

In `figure_sensitivity`, replace `s=38` with
`s=16 + 74 * np.sqrt(wind.loc[order, "wind_MW"] / wind["wind_MW"].max())`.

The square root is compression, not an area scale: marker area would go as
√MW, so dots would **rank** capacity but could not be read quantitatively.
Update the captions if you switch.

## What these figures do not show

- **Nothing here is validated against reality.** The flows were checked against
  PyPSA's independent DC solver (agreement `3.9 × 10⁻⁹ MW` across all 168
  snapshots), which proves our arithmetic, not our model.
- **Every time series behind panels 3 and the sign column is synthetic**,
  generated by the kit authors — a spatially-correlated wind field anchored to
  the TYTFS snapshot. No hour in it ever happened. See the kit's `README.md`
  LIMITATIONS section.
- **Ratings are TYTFS `RATE1` planning values**, not operational limits. "100%
  loaded" means 100% of a planning number.
- **No contingencies.** Intact base case only. Real network limits usually bind
  post-fault (N−1), so the true congested set would be larger.
